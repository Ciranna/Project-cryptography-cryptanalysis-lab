How does the Caesar Cipher work?

The Caesar cipher replaces each letter with another letter shifted by a fixed number of positions in the alphabet.

Encryption: E(x) = (x + k) mod 26

Decryption: D(y) = (y - k) mod 26

The key k determines the number of positions by which every letter is shifted.

Why does statistical cryptanalysis work?


            

                English letters do not occur equally often.
                For example, E is generally more frequent
                than Q or Z.
            


            

                The Caesar cipher shifts letter identities
                but preserves their occurrence counts.
                We can therefore test every key and compare
                the resulting letter distribution with
                typical English frequencies.
            


            

                A lower chi-squared score indicates a
                closer match to the expected distribution.
                It does not guarantee a correct decryption,
                especially for short ciphertexts.

Main Scientific Conclusion


        

        Caesar cryptanalysis exploits the statistical
        structure of natural language and a very small
        key space. Its automatic key identification
        becomes generally more reliable as the
        ciphertext length increases.
        


        

        The ECC brute-force attack exploits an
        artificially restricted private-key range.
        It does not depend on the statistical
        distribution of the plaintext.
        


        

        Properly generated secp256k1 private keys
        have an enormous search space, making
        exhaustive recovery computationally
        infeasible with current classical methods.
